# Choosing a reveal: annotated examples

Use these examples to select leads and shape stories. They teach judgment, not claims transferable to a new app. Reference videos present a creator's evidence and interpretation; investigate the target's own artifacts.

## Strong patterns to recognize

| Pattern | Concrete reveal | What to investigate next |
|---|---|---|
| Choice without control | A result is decided before a user picks one of several cards. | Outcome assignment, tap handler, displayed explanation, alternative choices and upsell path. |
| Apparent skill/luck changes | Retry history changes generated colors, prize weights or score increments. | Original/later attempts, counter units, consumers, resets and measured outcomes. Hidden help is interesting too. |
| Same label, different bargain | People receive different puzzles, prices or rewards under matching labels. | Effective rules, assignments, histories and what shared statistics measure. |
| Personal-looking feedback without its measurement | A moving multiplier follows elapsed time/randomness rather than the outcome's measurement. | Display provider, real metrics, paid product behavior and interface implications. |
| Conditional access | Already-available information is hidden behind activity or a paid-state bypass. | Loaded data, gate, free exits, experiment defaults and paid-state effects. |
| An economic loop | Early gifts fund an attempt; later gifts shrink while price stays, and retries have another rule. | Entry path, original draw, duplicates, grants, alternative retry routes and resets. |
| Collection leading to intervention | Behavior/context informs a prediction or selects re-engagement. | Read/serialization, model/segment output, notification/offer consumer and observations. |

## Learn from reference videos

**Charles — [Tinder](https://www.instagram.com/p/DemiE6LxXlk/), about 21–50 seconds.** Card outcomes and an animated Boost calculation change how viewers understand choice and feedback. Find the hidden provider controlling what the person thinks they control or measure.

**Charles — [Crossy Road](https://www.instagram.com/p/DeqQs8ztMKE/), about 11–57 seconds.** Gifts, spin price, duplicates and retry odds form one story. Explain how someone reaches a spin before its percentages. The underlying research found coin/ad retries share the improvement; keep retry effects distinct from ad-exclusive effects.

**Charles — [Candy Crush](https://www.instagram.com/p/DerlD7DxnCW/), about 9–44 seconds.** Color weights after repeated starts and different puzzles under one level number are the reveal. Config counts add scale. Color probabilities, board difficulty and measured win rates answer different questions.

**Ayoships — [Duolingo](https://www.instagram.com/p/DeUlQTQxv5O/), about 5–45 seconds.** The narrative connects return-probability fields, context and reminders. Collection becomes interesting through a possible intervention. Fields do not recover the full probability model, and notification rotation alone does not establish preference-based personalization.

**Ayoships — [Subway Surfers](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DenaJb2xyW-/), about 10–46 seconds.** Different shop prices/ad options provide the concrete payoff. Player attributes and server JSON establish where to dig. Keep property counts, experiment inventories and observed effective versions separate.

**Ayoships — [Block Blast](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DecfvksvHiW/), about 12–47 seconds.** The story links ad value, memorable traits, gameplay selection, loss and revival. Trace every connection. Its displayed score excerpt has a bounded random increment, not proof of an exact fixed ten-percent addition.

For new references use tracking/transcription tools and actual frames. Transcripts cannot establish displayed code. Reuse identity-checked captures. Study clarity and causal storytelling without inheriting allegations.

## Calibration from prior investigations

**Useful implemented-code finding: loaded Likes behind learning.** An experimental branch can withhold an already-loaded incoming list, bypass the gate for subscribers and offer free exits at activity/age thresholds. The hidden relationship between available data and access is compelling. Preserve defaults, exits and unobserved activation.

**Useful implemented-code finding: winning changes the pool.** A prize handler can reduce later weights and make another prize unavailable until reset. Follow grants, continuation/ad paths and resets. Explain the next opportunity; changing weights alone does not give final reel win percentages.

**Weak promotion: a broader sensor packet one time in ten.** Hidden collection is a lead. Find a meaningful privacy/destination/consent consequence or a connection to treatment before calling it game manipulation. Otherwise pursue a more consequential branch.

**Weak fallback: visible subscription defaults and preference badges.** Difficult override/personalization leads still call for relevant code/runtime work. A weekly default setter or fixed “95% prefer” value does not supply the missing hidden-rule result.

**Worthwhile partial lead: extra bonus cap, off in the guest.** Preserve base reward and identify the additional part that can disappear. Investigate classification, naturally delivered activation, other segments and resets. Offer a strong motive hypothesis while keeping activation unresolved.

## Compact examples of delivery

These illustrate phrasing with sample values; use the investigated app's actual values and evidence.

- “Retries get a better chance as the counter rises, and leaving resets that counter. We still need the original spin's odds.” The next guess can explain why abandoning accumulated retry progress might discourage leaving; it does not need to assume that the original spin was stingy.
- “Our two groups received different boards and move limits under Level 12. We have not measured which is harder or how the assignments were chosen. My best guess is that these alternatives help test what keeps people playing.” This retains the reveal without inventing random or spending-based assignment.
- “In this experiment, the app can hide Likes it has already loaded; subscribers bypass the gate. Our account did not demonstrate that branch.” Then explain the possible curiosity/activity/subscription incentive. Welcome-screen footage illustrates context, not an observed gate.
- “The cap can remove five extra coins while leaving ten base coins. It was off for our test player.” Keep its classification and activation question as a promising next investigation.

Explain the actual behavior a rule might encourage: another retry because leaving loses an advantage, more activity to reveal incoming interest, or reassurance that a paid feature works. Keep the rule, captured result, best guess and next test recognizable. Treat unknown starting odds, a shared statistic's validity and whether users know a hidden rule as questions rather than supplying convenient premises.
